Four things your circle does.

Everything happens between people who already know each other. Circles keeps the record.

  1. Form a circle

    Invite 5 to 20 friends, classmates or teammates. Each person verifies once with Aadhaar eKYC and a PAN check.

  2. Pool every month

    Everyone commits ₹500 to ₹5,000 a month. The money stays in each member's own bank account until it's lent. Circles only keeps the shared total.

  3. Vote on loans

    A member asks for ₹1,000 to ₹15,000 and says what it's for. When 70% of the circle votes yes, it's approved. Your circle can change that bar.

  4. Repay by UPI

    Lenders send by UPI straight to the borrower, and repayments come back the same way, with AutoPay if you want it. Every rupee lands in the shared ledger.

Circles never holds your money.

Money moves bank to bank over UPI, or through a bank-held escrow account. We keep the ledger.

  • Aadhaar eKYC and a PAN check for every member before they can lend or borrow.
  • A digital loan agreement and a Key Fact Statement before any money moves.
  • First loans are capped. The cap rises after every three on-time repayments.
  • Outside borrowers are assessed on bank statements, enrolment, UPI history and gig income.
  • Repayments on outside loans are reported to credit bureaus.
  • Every outside loan is split across many circles, so one default never lands on one group.
  • The ledger is reconciled with the escrow bank every day.
  • Circles is a Lending Service Provider working with a licensed NBFC-P2P partner, under RBI's P2P rules.

Lending carries risk. If a borrower doesn't repay, you can lose some or all of what you lent. Only lend what you can do without.

Questions people ask first.

Does Circles hold my money?

No. There is no Circles wallet. Money moves bank to bank over UPI, and outside loans go through an escrow account held by a bank-appointed trustee. Circles keeps the record of who owes what.

What if someone doesn't pay back?

Inside a circle, everyone sees a missed instalment in the ledger and the circle can vote to restructure the loan. Late payers keep a low cap. On outside loans, each loan is split across many circles and repayments are reported to credit bureaus. You can still lose money, so only lend what you can do without.

Is this legal?

Peer-to-peer lending in India is regulated by the RBI. Circles works as a Lending Service Provider alongside a licensed NBFC-P2P partner, with escrow, eKYC, digital agreements and a Key Fact Statement for every loan.

Who can join?

Anyone 18 or older with an Indian bank account on UPI, an Aadhaar number and a PAN. Most circles are friends, classmates, hostel blocks, teams and clubs.

How much can I borrow?

Inside a circle, ₹1,000 to ₹15,000 per loan. Your first loans are capped lower, and the cap rises after every three on-time repayments.
